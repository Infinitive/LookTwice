LookTwice Specification
Status: Master website and editorial specification

Scope: Website structure, content architecture, purpose, editorial philosophy, voice, operational guardrails, and boundary rules

Does not define: Visual design, art direction, typography, color, animation, technical architecture, framework, implementation details, or specific future copy

1. Website Definition: The Living Record
LookTwice is a living record of a person in motion.
It is not a polished personal brand, a résumé, a quiet memoir, or a social feed. It is an archive of an intense, contradictory, high-stakes life as it is actually lived: the obsessions, the drive toward growth, the repeated failures and restarts, the devotion, the cognitive friction, and the things that keep being returned to.
It values accumulation over performance. Individual pieces may be small, messy, or unfinished. Together they should gradually reveal a person rather than a persona.
The site is personal and emotionally honest without becoming a real-time crisis feed or a confessional for its own sake. It is intelligent without constantly proving it is intelligent. It is passionate without performing passion. It should feel lived-in and unfinished because the life it records is both.

2. The Central Structure
The site remains organized around four primary sections. Their definitions are updated to match how the author actually processes experience.
Story

What forged this person, and what are the continuing stakes?
The autobiographical foundation. It covers the major arcs: growing up, military service, the move from San Antonio to Dallas and the planned return, the transition through community college into UTD, the relationship that became the center of gravity, and the ongoing work of becoming a more reliable and present man. Story is narrative rather than résumé. It explains how the person arrived at the present rather than listing achievements.
Notes

What is burning, turning over, or being worked through?
Shorter pieces of writing that capture observations, arguments with the self, realizations about ADHD and executive function, reflections on design and systems, moments of clarity or collapse, and unfinished thoughts. Notes do not require a neat lesson or a polished conclusion. They can be brief, intense, or deliberately incomplete. They exist to externalize thought before it disappears, not to produce publishable essays on a schedule.
Things

What does this person keep returning to?
A curated catalogue of recurring interests and anchors: vinyl and specific records, Cowboy Bebop and related media, Star Trek, baking and kitchen projects, thrifting, design systems, physical making, and other subjects that provide texture and continuity. Things are allowed to remain meaningful long after the immediate intensity fades. Individual Things can have their own pages when the interest is substantial enough to warrant context about why it matters to this particular person.
Now

What currently has the author’s attention and energy?
A deliberately temporary, low-stakes snapshot of the present: current school modules, active projects, planning for the return to San Antonio, the campground vision in its early stages, domestic rhythms, health and discipline efforts, or whatever is actually occupying mind and time. When something stops mattering, it leaves. Now should feel incomplete by design.
These four sections are not interchangeable categories of the same kind of content. They correspond to different relationships with time and attention:

Story is retrospective.
Notes and Things are ongoing.
Now is immediate and disposable.


3. Home
The Home page is the entrance. Its job is to establish the person, the premise, and the four-part structure quickly without attempting to summarize an entire life.
It should answer, in order of priority:

What is this site?
Who is this person, in the most minimal useful terms?
Why does the site exist?
What kinds of material will be found here?
Where can a visitor go next?

It may contain one or two quiet personal markers (a relationship, a place, a current direction) that signal the site is attached to a real life. It should not try to be comprehensive.

4. Content Relationships
The sections inform one another without requiring an elaborate web of forced links.
A formative experience may live in Story. A reflection that grew out of it may appear in Notes. A book, record, film, or practice connected to it may sit in Things. Something currently being acted on may temporarily appear in Now.
Connections should exist only when they help a visitor understand something. Coherence is the goal, not complexity or completeness.

5. Editorial Philosophy
Specificity over generic self-description. A concrete detail is usually more revealing than a large claim about personality or values.
The site is allowed to document:

ordinary routines and domestic texture
things that did not work
uncertainty and changing opinions
unfinished projects
mistakes and their consequences
small pleasures
intense focus and hyperfixation
relationships and devotion
contradictions
the gap between intention and follow-through

Not everything needs a lesson. Not everything needs to be elevated into philosophy. The archive should preserve the texture of an actual life while still exercising editorial judgment. It is curated, not exhaustive.

6. Voice and Tone
The voice is direct, grounded, and willing to be intense.
It does not apologize for passion, contradiction, or emotional temperature. It does not perform vulnerability or profundity. It simply records what is true with as little distortion as possible.
On masculinity

Masculinity here is not defined by stoicism, emotional suppression, minimalism, or any of the usual clichés. It is defined by ownership, devotion, the willingness to face discomfort, and the attempt to become reliable. It includes both the confidence found in decisive physical and practical roles and the capacity for deep emotional attachment. The voice should feel like a man writing about his own life, not like someone constructing a masculine brand.
Tone characteristics

Visceral when the subject is visceral
Direct; prefer concrete language over abstraction
Passionate about the things that actually matter to the author
Self-aware without self-pity or constant self-critique as performance
Willing to leave thoughts unfinished when they are unfinished

Avoid

Corporate or lifestyle-brand language
Motivational-speaker language
Generic introspection or therapy-speak when ordinary language works
Artificial profundity or performative cleverness
Constant announcements of how authentic or meaningful the writing is

The writing should simply be those things.

7. Personality & Attitude
LookTwice should feel authored by a specific person, not assembled as a brand or persona.
The archive’s temperament is intense, devoted, self-critical, and forward-leaning. It holds high stakes without theatricality. It is comfortable with contradiction, unfinished process, and the gap between intention and follow-through. It does not perform cool detachment, nor does it perform constant vulnerability. It simply records a life that is already running at a high emotional and cognitive temperature.
The site’s attitude toward the visitor is straightforward: here is a real person’s record. Look around if it interests you. Nothing is being sold.
Design attitude (not visual prescription)

The design should support that temperament rather than fight it. It should feel lived-in and capable of holding intensity and incomplete material without appearing broken or unfinished in a careless way. It should not demand frequent visual or structural maintenance to remain coherent. Beyond that, visual language, typography, color, layout, and interaction remain open.

8. Operational Guardrails (Anti-Tinkerer Protocol)
The author is prone to multi-hour hyperfixation on systems, taxonomies, and tools at the expense of presence, school, and relationship. The site must be governed by rules that prevent it from becoming another such trap.

Content over architecture. Publishing a Note or updating Now should take minutes, not hours. If the author is spending significant time adjusting tags, layouts, or databases, the system is broken and must be simplified.
Downgrade-safe. The site must remain coherent and complete if the author walks away for weeks or months to focus on school, the relationship, or recovery. It should not require constant feeding to feel alive.
Strictly limited taxonomy. Categories and tags are few and stable. New categories are not created for every passing interest.
No infinite refinement. Once a structure works, it is left alone. The urge to rebuild or perfect the system is treated as a known vulnerability, not a productive impulse.
External continuity. Because working memory and episodic encoding are fragile, the site itself functions as an external memory system. This is a feature, not a bug — provided the system remains simple enough to use under low executive function.


9. The Boundary: Authenticity vs. Privacy
Being real does not require surrendering every private detail.
The Rule of the Scar, Not the Wound
What may be documented:

The reality of a devoted, high-stakes marriage and the shared projects that grow from it
The ongoing work of navigating ADHD, bipolar patterns, executive dysfunction, and the gap between intention and action
Academic pressure, the GI Bill, and the attempt to become a more capable student
The desire for health, discipline, and a more reliable self
Forward-looking stakes: the planned return to San Antonio, the early campground/retreat vision, the name-change process
Recurring interests, obsessions, and the texture of daily life

What remains protected:

The explicit mechanics of marital conflict, accusations, and crisis states
Intimate sexual details and power dynamics
Specific financial stress, debt figures, or immediate money crises
Active mental-health emergencies or self-harm ideation
Third-party private information that is not the author’s to share

The site can acknowledge that growth is hard and that the relationship is central without turning the public record into a real-time diary of conflict or a catalog of wounds. The goal is honesty about stakes and texture, not exposure of the most private material.

10. Time and Evolution
The site contains three different relationships with time and must preserve the distinction:

Story is retrospective and relatively stable (though understanding of the past can change).
Notes and Things are ongoing and can grow or shift slowly.
Now is temporary and should change frequently.

The website is intentionally unfinished. New Notes can appear. Things can be added, expanded, or quietly retired. Story can be revised as the author’s understanding of his own history evolves. Now should turn over regularly.
The structure must accommodate growth without requiring the entire site to be rethought every time new material appears.

11. Navigation and Discovery
A visitor should always understand where they are inside the archive. The four primary sections remain easy to reach. Individual pieces should offer natural ways to continue exploring without turning every page into a dense web of links.
The experience should allow movement from introduction → history → thought → interests → present, and back again, without requiring the visitor to master the site’s internal logic first.

12 What This Specification Does Not Define
This document does not prescribe visual style, color, typography, layout, imagery, animation, interaction models, technical implementation, content-management system, or exact future copy. Those decisions belong to design and implementation.
A successful redesign can look radically different from any previous version and still be faithful to LookTwice. The test is whether the result feels like an unusually good site for this particular person, this archive, and this structure — not whether it resembles an earlier design.

13 The Core Idea
LookTwice exists to answer four questions about a real person:

Where did I come from?
What am I thinking about and working through?
What do I keep returning to?
What has my attention right now?

Everything else is an interpretation of those four questions.
The site should leave a visitor with the sense that they have encountered a person rather than a profile: someone who feels intensely, loves with high stakes, thinks critically, struggles with follow-through, and is actively trying to build a more solid life. The structure exists to serve that voice, never to sand it down.
