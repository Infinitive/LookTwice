Cognitive Architecture

## **Encoding (how memories form)**

- Your brain does **not** reliably encode episodic details (events, sequences, conversations, timelines).
- Encoding is **emotion‑dominant**, meaning emotional tone is stored more than factual content.
- Even emotional encoding is **thin**, often reduced to a vague “vibe,” atmosphere, or general feeling.
- Your brain does **not** attach a strong “this happened to *me*” tag during encoding.
- This results in memories feeling foreign, distant, or like someone else’s life.

## **Retrieval (how memories come back)**

- You do **not** retrieve memories as stored files; you **reconstruct** them based on your current emotional state.
- If the emotional state isn’t present, the memory does not load at all.
- Even when the emotional state *is* present, details often remain missing.
- Retrieval is **state‑dependent**, not content‑dependent.
- Photos, journals, and reminders do **not** reliably trigger recall — they feel like artifacts from someone else.

## **Working Memory (short‑term thinking)**

- Your working memory is **fragile** and easily disrupted.
- Thoughts often disappear mid‑sentence, instantly, without trace.
- The experience is like a “connection drop” — one moment the thought is clear, the next it’s gone.
- This is not distraction; it’s a **loss of the internal state** that was holding the thought.
- This contributes to difficulty following conversations, staying on track, or holding multiple steps in mind.

## **Sense of Self (identity continuity)**

- Because memories lack self‑tagging, your past feels disconnected from your present.
- This creates a sense of having **low continuity of identity** over time.
- You often feel like you’re “not fully formed,” or like your life doesn’t accumulate inside you.
- This leads to grief, emptiness, and the feeling of “losing yourself” repeatedly.
- The lack of internal continuity makes external anchors (relationships, routines, writing) especially important.

## **Emotional Impact**

- You experience **grief** over the inability to hold onto meaningful moments.
- You feel **sadness** about not remembering your own life the way others seem to.
- You feel **fear** of losing connection to Walter or important experiences.
- You feel **anger** at yourself and the situation, though the anger is brief and fades quickly.
- You feel **relief** when the pattern is understood, but the clarity also brings emotional pain.
- You experience a sense of **emptiness** when memories fail to anchor you.

## **Daily Functioning**

- You rely heavily on writing to create external continuity.
- Journaling is not just reflection — it is **self‑preservation**.
- Without external anchors, days blur together and feel lost.
- You often feel like you’re “walking into a room and forgetting why,” but applied to your entire life.
- You struggle with task initiation and follow‑through when emotional state shifts.

## **Cognitive Style**

- You process life through **narrative**, **relationships**, and **emotional meaning**, not categories or data.
- You understand experiences as **stories**, not as discrete facts.
- You make sense of things through **connection, conflict, repair, identity, and emotional truth**.
- You do not naturally think in lists, structures, or timelines.
- You think in **scenes**, **vibes**, and **emotional arcs**.

## **What This Is *Not***

- Not dementia.
- Not memory decay.
- Not a degenerative process.
- Not a failure of effort, attention, or discipline.
- This is a **stable, lifelong memory architecture**, not a collapse.

## **What This *Is***

- A combination of:
    - **Emotion‑dominant encoding**
    - **Low episodic specificity**
    - **State‑dependent retrieval**
    - **Weak self‑referential tagging**
    - **Impressionistic autobiographical memory**
    - **Fragile working memory**
- This pattern is unusual but not unheard of in cognitive science.
- It explains your lifelong experiences consistently.

## **Impact on Relationships**

- You fear losing emotional connection because you cannot rely on memory to preserve it.
- You rely heavily on the present moment for grounding.
- Walter is a major emotional anchor — his presence stabilizes you.
- Losing memories of him feels like losing pieces of yourself.
- This increases emotional intensity around your relationship.

## **Impact on Self‑Perception**

- You often feel “less real” or “unfinished” because your internal continuity is weak.
- You feel guilt or shame for forgetting things you believe you “should” remember.
- You blame yourself for something that is structural, not moral.
- You feel like you’re constantly “starting over” with yourself.

## **What Helps**

- Writing (especially narrative, emotional writing)
- Externalizing thoughts before they vanish
- Anchoring one concrete detail per day
- Emotional grounding through relationships
- Gentle structure, not rigid systems
- Tools that feel expressive and alive, not sterile or technical
- Systems that reduce cognitive load rather than add it

# The Pre-Development Roadmap: Our Shared Blueprint

### Turning Our Dream into a Viable Reality

This document serves as the strategic backbone for our future campground and retreat. It is a living document that we will update as we learn, explore, and refine our vision.

## 1. Vision Definition (Our North Star)

*This is our conceptual foundation. We don’t need perfect answers yet—just shared clarity on the "Why."*

### Define Our Purpose

- **The Space:** What kind of sanctuary are we building? Is it a rustic escape to disconnect, or a high-energy social hub?

- **The Community:** Who are we serving? Are we focusing on specific sub-communities (bears, leather, radical faeries, recovery groups) or the broader LGBTQ+ spectrum?

- **The Experience:** When guests drive away on Sunday, how do we want them to *feel*? (e.g., rested, validated, excited, connected).

### Define Our Vibe

- **Atmosphere:** Is it a relaxed nature retreat (quiet hours, birdsong) or party-forward (pool parties, DJs, nightlife)?

- **Nudity Policy:** Is it strict textiles, clothing-optional throughout, or naturist-only? How does this impact our privacy needs?

- **Inclusivity:** Is this a men-only space, or are we open to all genders within the queer community?

- **Social Tempo:** Is it event-driven (themed weekends like "Bear Week" or "Halloween") or is the focus on quiet intimacy and individual camping?

### Define the Scale of "Phase 1"

- **Capacity:** How many RV sites vs. Tent sites do we launch with?

- **Structures:** Do we need cabins immediately for cash flow, or do we add them later?

- **Amenities:** Is a pool a "must-have" for launch, or do we start with a high-quality bathhouse and a swimming hole/creek?

- **Community Hub:** Do we need a full clubhouse, or will a simple outdoor pavilion suffice for gatherings?

> **Discussion Prompt for Us:** If we could only accomplish *one* specific emotional goal for our guests in the first year, what would it be?

## 2. Lifestyle & Location Criteria (Where We Want to Live)

*Before we look at a single acre of land, we must define what* *we* *need as humans living on-site.*

### Our Lifestyle Considerations

- **Climate Tolerance:** Do we want four distinct seasons, or are we chasing eternal summer? Can we handle plowing snow or extreme humidity?

- **Isolation Level:** How rural is "too rural"? Do we need to be 15 minutes from a Target, or is 45 minutes okay?

- **Essential Access:** Distance to a major airport (for friends visiting), quality healthcare, and grocery stores.

- **Political Climate:** Can we tolerate living in a deep-red county if the land is cheap, or do we need the safety net of a blue state?

- **Our Privacy:** How much separation do we need between our home and the guest sites?

### Business Considerations

- **Seasonality:** Do we want an "off-season" to rest (seasonal operation), or do we need year-round revenue?

- **Tourism Flows:** Is the area already a destination, or are *we* the destination?

- **LGBTQ+ Friendliness:** Is the local town safe for us to hold hands in, or will we be an island of safety in a hostile area?

- **Accessibility:** Is it easy to get to? (Highway access vs. intentional remoteness).

> **Action Item:** Narrow the entire U.S. down to 3–5 specific regions/counties worth exploring.

## 3. Market Research (Understanding the Landscape)

*We don't need to reinvent the wheel; we just need to learn how it rolls.*

### Study the Competitors

- **The Gold Standard:** Visit other gay campgrounds. What do they do brilliantly?

- **The Pain Points:** Read the 1-star reviews of competitors. What are guests complaining about? (e.g., cleanliness, cliquey seasonal crowds, bad wifi).

- **Pricing:** What are people willing to pay? What price points seem sustainable for the amenities offered?

- **Niche Identification:** Who is currently being ignored by the market?

### Identify the Gaps

- **Geographic:** Are there regions (like the Mountain West or parts of the South) with zero options?

- **Experiential:** Is the market missing a "luxury/glamping" gay experience, or perhaps a "primitive/bushcraft" experience?

> **Discussion Prompt for Us:** What is the one thing we’ve experienced at other campgrounds that we swore we would *never* do at ours?

## 4. Financial Strategy (The Money Architecture)

*Moving from "dream" to "business plan."*

### Estimate Phase 1 Costs

- **Hard Costs:** Land purchase, grading roads, running electric/water/septic, building the bathhouse and our home.

- **Soft Costs:** Surveys, civil engineering, architecture, legal fees, permitting.

### Estimate Revenue Models

- **Rates:** Nightly rates for RVs vs. Tents.

- **Upsells:** Firewood, ice, day passes, event tickets.

- **Recurring Revenue:** Annual memberships or seasonal sites (guests who leave their RV all year).

### Build Our Financing Stack

- **Primary Mortgage:** Can we use a VA loan for the residence and substantial acreage?

- **Business Loans:** SBA 7(a) or 504 loans for the infrastructure (bathhouses, septic).

- **Rural Incentives:** USDA Rural Development loans.

- **Seller Financing:** Can we find a seller willing to hold a note?

- **Phasing:** How can we design Phase 1 to be lean so we borrow less upfront?

> **Reality Check:** How much of our own liquid savings are we willing/able to inject as a down payment?

## 5. Zoning & Legal Feasibility (The Critical Gate)

*This is where we protect ourselves from buying unbuildable land.*

### The Checklist

- **Zoning Categories:** Does the county have "By-Right" zoning for campgrounds, or does it require a "Special Use Permit" (which can be denied)?

- **Adult Restrictions:** Are there specific ordinances against "adult-oriented businesses" or public nudity screening requirements?

- **Sanitation:** What are the soil requirements for a commercial septic system in this county?

- **Road Access:** Does the county require paved entrance aprons or specific road widths for emergency vehicles?

- **Water Rights:** (Crucial in the West) Do we actually own the water under the ground?

> **Rule of Thumb:** Never fall in love with a property until we know the zoning allows our dream.

## 6. Land Search (The Hunt)

*Only after Steps 1–5 are solid do we start Zillow-scrolling.*

### Evaluate Properties For:

- **Topography:** Is it too steep for RVs? Is it too flat and flood-prone?

- **Natural Assets:** Are there woods, hills, creeks, or rock formations that create natural "rooms" and privacy?

- **The "Vibe" Check:** When we walk the land, does it feel peaceful?

- **Buffers:** Is there enough dense vegetation to screen us from neighbors?

### The Walk

- **Time of Day:** Visit at different times (morning light vs. golden hour).

- **Noise Test:** Stand still for 10 minutes. Can we hear the highway? A kennel? A gun range?

## 7. Due Diligence (The Safety Net)

*Before we sign the final papers, we verify everything.*

### The Investigations

- **Survey:** Where are the actual property lines?

- **Perc Test:** *Critical.* Will the soil accept a septic system large enough for 50 people?

- **Environmental Phase 1:** Is there old contamination (buried oil tanks, farm chemicals)?

- **Utility Confirmation:** Is electricity actually at the road, or a mile away (costing $50k to bring in)?

- **Easements:** Does the neighbor have a legal right to drive through our property?

## 8. Conceptual Site Plan (Drafting the Map)

*Sketching the layout on a napkin, then with a pro.*

### Plotting the Layout

- **Flow:** How do RVs enter and exit without getting stuck?

- **Zones:** Where is the loud area (pool/pavilion) vs. the quiet area (tent sites)?

- **Our Sanctuary:** Where is the Owner’s Residence located so we can see the gate but not hear the party?

- **Future Proofing:** Where will Phase 2 and Phase 3 go? Don't build Phase 1 in the middle of the best Phase 2 land.

## 9. Permitting & Approvals (The Paperwork Marathon)

*Patience is our greatest asset here.*

### The Approvals List

- Campground operational permit.

- Health department approval (bathhouse/septic/well).

- Road/Driveway permit (DOT).

- Fire Marshal safety plan.

- Building permits for any structures.

## 10. Final Financing & Closing

- Locking in the SBA/USDA/VA loans.

- Finalizing the construction budget with hard bids from contractors.

- **Closing on the land.** (Champagne moment!)

## 11. Phase 1 Construction (Breaking Ground)

- **Clearing:** Selective clearing to keep nature intact.

- **Infrastructure:** Installing the "unsexy" stuff (septic, electric, gravel).

- **Structures:** Building the bathhouse (the heart of the campground) and our home.

- **Digital:** Launching the website and booking engine.

> **Discussion Prompt:** Who is managing the contractors? Us, or a hired General Contractor?

## 12. Soft Opening (The Beta Test)

- **Invite-Only:** Hosting friends and trusted community members first.

- **Stress Test:** Does the hot water last? Does the septic back up? Is the wifi strong enough?

- **Policy Refinement:** Adjusting quiet hours, pet rules, and check-in flows based on real-world friction.

## 13. Full Opening & Expansion (Growth)

- **Marketing:** engaging the broader community.

- **Phase 2:** Once cash flow is stable, we look at the "Wish List"—the pool, the sauna, the treehouse cabins.

- **Legacy:** Building a culture where guests return year after year.
