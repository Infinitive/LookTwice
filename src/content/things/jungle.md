On September 26, 2026, I saw Jungle live with my husband and a friend of ours, who bought the tickets. The invitation was unexpected and deeply appreciated. I likely would not have seen them otherwise, certainly not for a while.

Jungle already occupied a specific musical space for me: funk, modern disco, neo-soul, organic dance, music engineered around rhythm without feeling mechanical. I had always been captivated by their sound and their exceptional music videos. Live, that polish became physical. Our friend danced through the entire show. The room moved together. 

I noted that I tried very hard to make the day memorable. That sentence now carries more weight than I intended. The concert belonged to a complicated weekend, one containing joy, hardship, distraction, and conflict afterward. Preserving only the clean version would be dishonest. Allowing everything that followed to erase the show would be dishonest too.

A life is rarely organized enough to give its best moments uncontaminated space. The concert did not arrive after every problem had been solved. It arrived inside the problems.

My husband enjoyed the performance even though large events take more out of him now than they once did. His social battery is different from mine, and our social life is usually shared by design. That detail matters because the memory is not simply about seeing a band. It is about who stood beside me, who made it possible, and the compromises involved in experiencing it together.

The music gave the evening a center. For a while there was no project to finish, self to repair, or future to plan. There was rhythm, movement, and the fact of being there. There was the simple joy of an experience so encompassing it, however temporarily, makes all the wrongs in the world disappear. 

That is enough reason to keep it.
